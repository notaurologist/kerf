Number entry and form states

How a length or an angle is typed into Kerf, how Kerf shows what it understood, and what every form looks like while it loads, waits, fails, and saves.

Today the editor takes decimal inches only (138.375) and echoes them in feet and inches. A woodworker reads a tape as feet, inches, and sixteenths, and writes 138-3/8. This page compares three ways to enter a length, proposes a standard for what Kerf accepts, and draws the form states every direction shares. The mockups use #42's typeface and #43's C1 colors; every echoed length on this page is a string from core's formatLength.

What I need from you

Three choices. I suggest E1, errors on save, and the standard as written; each is listed first and marked "Suggested" below. A reply like E1, errors on save, standard as written is enough.

  1. How a length is entered: E1 typed as written on a plan, E2 split fields, or E3 Kerf's own keypad on a phone.
  2. When problems are shown: errors on save, with Kerf's reading shown while you type, or errors as soon as you leave a field.
  3. The entry standard: approve what Kerf accepts and refuses, and that core does the parsing, or change it.

Try E1's field on your phone: it runs a prototype of the parser. The light and dark switch above the directions applies to every mockup.

Common to all three

What every direction does

Core reads, core echoes

Whatever the control, core turns the entry into decimal inches and formats it back. The echo under the field is core's reading, in feet and inches and then inches, as the plan will show it (hard rules 1 and 6).

Exact until display

What is typed is kept exactly. If it is not a whole number of sixteenths, the echo says it is shown to the nearest 1/16 and that Kerf keeps the exact value (hard rule 4).

No sample values

Hints describe the format, never a plausible number for that field, so nothing on the screen can pass for a default (hard rule 3). Placeholders are not used: they vanish while typing and are hard to read (GOV.UK).

Labels carry the reference

Every length says where it is measured from ("above the ground under the beam"), and angles say what they are measured from ("from vertical"). Saw settings are never typed; they are outputs.

Never color alone

An error has an icon, a message, and a heavier border; input needed has a dashed outline and its words (#43, rules 4 and 6).

Text fields, not number fields

type="number" cannot hold a fraction such as 3/8, and GOV.UK advises against it unless research shows a need. Lengths are text fields with autocorrect, capitals, and spelling checks turned off.

Directions

Three ways to enter a length

The worked example's frame: beam 120" long at 96", legs at 20° from vertical, 12" inset. The editor is used mostly on the laptop (#40: Edit opens the split screen), and planning screens are the only place parameters change. A phone is the second case.

E1 · Typed, as written on a plan · Suggested

One field per length; type it the way a tape or a plan writes it, and Kerf shows what it read

Such as 138-3/8, 11' 6-3/8", or 138.4.

Runs a prototype of the proposed parser with core's own formatLength, copied into this page.

From the subject
How lengths are written on plans and typed into drawing programs. CAD tools set to architectural units read a plain number as inches and take feet with an apostrophe and a hyphenated fraction (3'1-5/8); SketchUp's measurements box takes 2'6 or 3 1/4. A woodworker already writes 138-3/8 on the board.
Principles
Forgiving format (Tidwell, Designing Interfaces): accept the forms people use and let the program do the work. GOV.UK: accept different formats "as long as it's not ambiguous", and ignore stray spaces. WCAG 3.3.2 and 3.3.3: say what is expected, and when something cannot be read, say how to fix it. The echo puts core's reading beside the text, so a slip like 183 for 138 shows as 15' 3" before it is saved.
Ambiguity is refused, not guessed: 11 6 could be 11' 6" or a missed key, so Kerf asks for the foot mark.
In use
Laptop, where the editor is used (#40: L-A split editor): the fastest of the three, since every mark is on the keyboard, and a length can be pasted from a plan or a note. Phone: works, but opens on letters; one tap to the numbers layer, where -, /, ', ", and . all sit. The field turns off autocorrect and capitals, and the parser also accepts the curly quotes a phone's smart punctuation types.
Measured
Keys to enter 138-3/8: 7 on a laptop, 8 taps on a phone (one to switch layers). 11'6-3/8: 8 keys, 9 taps.
Cost
A parser in core with hand-checked cases (about 50 already in the prototype on this page). The phone keyboard starts on letters, and the numbers layer has small keys.

E2 · Split fields

Feet, inches, and a fraction menu: nothing to parse, nothing to learn

From the subject
Structured format (Tidwell): one control per part of the value. GOV.UK asks for dates this way, as three fields for day, month, and year, because people know a date in parts. A tape is read in parts too: feet, inches, sixteenths.
Principles
Nothing to parse and nothing to learn. Each field gets the large number pad (inputmode="numeric"), and the fraction menu only offers sixteenths, so no fraction can be wrong.
In use
Phone: big keys, no layer switching. Laptop: slower; three controls per length, 14 lengths, so 42 stops for Tab, and choosing 3/8 from the menu by keyboard is six arrow presses. Copy and paste of a length does not work.
It cannot hold every length. A decimal like 138.4, or 1/32, has no place in a sixteenths menu, so editing a saved project either rounds it on the next save, which breaks hard rule 4 (exact until display), or needs a second way to enter decimals.
Measured
Keys to enter 138-3/8: about 11 on a laptop (Tab, 3 digits, Tab, 6 arrows), 7 or 8 taps on a phone (spinning the menu to 3/8 takes one or two flicks). 11'6-3/8: about 11 keys, 8 or 9 taps.
Cost
A grouped control for every length (fieldset, legend, three labels), a decimal fallback, and a form layout three times as wide per length.

E3 · Kerf's keypad

E1's typed field, with Kerf's own feet-inch-fraction keypad on a phone

From the subject
Construction calculators have keys for feet, inches, and the fraction bar, and their manuals have you enter "largest to smallest": 5 feet 1-1/2 inch is 5 f 1 i 1 / 2. E3 puts those keys on the phone: the page draws its own keypad and tells the phone not to show its keyboard.
Principles
The same parser and echo as E1, with each mark one tap away. Fitts's law favors the large keys of a 4-column pad over the 10-column numbers layer.
In use
Phone: the fewest taps, the largest keys. Laptop: identical to E1. Parameters are not entered at the store or in the garage, though: shop modes change progress only, never parameters (#40). The keypad speeds up a screen that is mostly used on the laptop.
A page-drawn keypad replaces something the phone does well: VoiceOver typing, dictation, a paired keyboard, the cursor and selection gestures, paste. Each has to keep working or be rebuilt, and #45's accessibility checks would cover a new kind of control.
Measured
Taps to enter 138-3/8: 7. 11'6-3/8: 8.
Cost
E1's parser plus a keypad component with its own focus, cursor, and screen-reader behavior, and tests for each. One tap saved per length against E1.

When problems are shown

Errors on save, or when you leave a field

The two main sources disagree. GOV.UK: "Do not validate when the user moves away from a field. Wait until they try to move to the next part." NN/g: show an error once the user "has finished with the field and moved to the next field", never while typing. Both agree that typing is not the time for errors.

Errors on save, reading while typingSuggested

While typing, the echo shows core's reading, or "Not a length yet" in the normal text color. Nothing turns crimson until Save, when every problem is marked beside its field and listed in a summary at the top that takes focus. The live echo already gives the early feedback NN/g is after, without calling a half-typed value wrong. The preview (#56) waits on fields it cannot read and names them.

Errors when you leave a field

A field is marked as soon as focus leaves it. Quicker to notice on a long form, but tabbing past an empty required field to fill another first marks it as an error, and on the edit screen the output beside it is already changing.

Where parsing lives

In core, beside the formatters: parseLength and parseAngle, used by buildFromForm, which already turns form text into values in core. The API keeps taking numbers, so nothing changes on the server or in stored snapshots. The messages come from core too, so a later screen or the API could reuse them.

Round trip

When a saved project is edited, each length is written back into its field as core formats it in inches (138-3/8) if it is a whole number of sixteenths, and as the decimal otherwise (138.4). Saving without a change never rounds a value.

Ambiguity is refused

Two numbers with no marks (11 6) could be feet and inches or a typo; the message asks for the foot mark. A number on its own is always inches, as in CAD tools set to architectural units.

Tape fractions

Denominators 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64. A third or a tenth is not on a tape, so it is more likely a slip than a measurement.

Angles

Decimal degrees, with or without ° or "deg": 20, 20.5°. Degrees and minutes are refused with "Enter decimal degrees, such as 20.5." The echo is core's formatAngle plus the field's reference ("20.0° from vertical").

Other numbers

Weights, percentages, and factors stay decimal. A template can let a number field take fractions where people write them that way, such as the ground slope (1/4 inch per foot).

Ranges in the user's units

Out-of-range messages give the limits as core formats them: "Beam height must be from 5' 0" to 12' 0"." The limits stay in the template's schema.

Metric

Refused with a message. Kerf is imperial throughout (DOMAIN.md, Units).

New requirements

What this stage adds

  • Core parses lengths and angles from text, with hand-checked tests, and buildFromForm uses it. Out-of-range messages give limits in feet and inches. A new issue, needed before #45 rebuilds the editor.
  • The editor writes saved values back into fields without rounding (the round trip above). Part of the same issue.
  • Form states as components in #45: field with echo, error, input needed, error summary, saving button, saved line, connection notice, leave-with-changes prompt, loading.
  • The "on this phone, not yet sent" mark for queued progress: already #57.

Forms and errors

GOV.UK Design System: text inputs (no type="number"; inputmode for keypads), error messages beside the field that say how to fix it, an error summary at the top that takes focus, and validation that waits for the user to submit and accepts any unambiguous format.

Error timing

NN/g: show an error once the user has finished with a field, next to it, and not while they are still typing.

Input patterns

Tidwell, Designing Interfaces (O'Reilly): Forgiving Format and Structured Format, the patterns behind E1 and E2.

IxDF: forgiving formats

How CAD tools read lengths

Architectural units: a plain number is inches, feet take an apostrophe, a fraction joins inches with a hyphen (3'1-5/8). SketchUp takes 2'6, 3 1/4, and an optional inch mark.

Triton College: number input conventions · SketchUp: the Measurements box · SketchUp forum: smart quotes break feet and inches

Construction calculators

Feet, inch, and fraction-bar keys, entered largest to smallest: 5 feet 1-1/2 inch is 5 f 1 i 1 / 2 (Construction Master Pro Trig user's guide, "Entering Dimensions", p. 18).

Keyboards

inputmode picks the on-screen keyboard: numeric is digits only, decimal adds the decimal separator, none shows none, for a page that draws its own.

MDN: inputmode

Accessibility

WCAG 2.2: 3.3.1 error identification, 3.3.2 labels or instructions, 3.3.3 error suggestion, and 4.1.3 status messages (the saved line and the summary are announced without taking focus; the echo is tied to its field with aria-describedby rather than announced on every key).
