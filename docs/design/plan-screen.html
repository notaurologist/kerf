Kerf · Design milestone · Issue #147

The plan screen, one part at a time

The plan is one long page of eight sections in order A: summary, diagrams, structural review, cut list, boards, shopping list, build steps, and parameters and inputs (#40, built in #119). With all eight built, it takes a lot of scrolling, and even with the contents beside it, it is hard to find the part you want and stay on it.

This page draws three ways to show one part at a time, each on the laptop and the phone, and answers the same six questions for each: how you move, what the address is, how links between sections work, how Edit fits, how it prints, and what a screen reader hears. Then it compares them and recommends one. The numbers in the sketches are the worked example as stored (template version 12, example 1).

Laptop frames are 900 px wide. On a phone, scroll them sideways inside their box.

What to choose

  1. Direction. P1 one section per screen, P2 the summary as a hub with five views named by question, or P3 one page with the sections closed. Suggested: P1, with each section's one-line digest from P2 added to the summary.
  2. The contents on a phone. a a bar naming the section, which opens the list, or b the list at the top of every section. Suggested: a.
  3. Diagrams and the cut list. a stay two sections, with the selected part carried in the address, or b merge with Boards into one section. Suggested: a, which keeps order A.

Once you choose, the choice and the rejected directions go in docs/design/README.md, and a follow-up issue holds the build.

Fixed

What stays the same in every direction

  • IA-2. The project is the plan. Shopping and Building stay full-screen modes, launched from the summary's cards, with the mode bar and Exit. No direction brings back IA-1's Plan, Shop, and Build tabs.
  • Order A. The sections keep their order, on screen and on paper. Print gives the whole plan in order A from wherever you are, with every "Show the working" open.
  • Edit. Edit still opens the separate edit screen (L-A), whose preview shows the margins, cut list, and shopping list with changes marked (#56). What changes is where Save brings you back to.
  • Numbers. Every number on screen comes from the stored snapshot through core. The section digests in P2 and P3 (counts such as "9 pieces from 7 boards") would come from core beside Plan.keyResults, not be counted in the web app.

Direction P1

One section per screen

Each of the eight sections is its own screen. The contents become the way to move: beside the plan on the laptop, and a bar at the top on the phone that names where you are and opens the list. Previous and Next at the end of each section name the sections either side. This is how GOV.UK's multi-part guides work: a numbered contents list, one part per page, Next naming the next part, and a printable version of the whole guide.

Getting there
  • Laptop: one click on any section in the contents; one click for Previous or Next. The contents stay put, with the current section marked.
  • Phone: one tap for Next or Previous; two taps for any other section (the bar, then the entry).
  • Back to the summary: one click on the laptop, two taps on the phone, or the browser's Back.
Address
  • One route per section: #/projects/<id> is the summary, then #/projects/<id>/structural-review, …/cut-list, and so on. The slugs cannot be edit, shop, or build, which the modes already use.
  • Moving between sections adds a history entry, so Back returns to the section before and a reload stays put. Selecting a part replaces the entry, so it does not fill the history.
Links between sections
  • A part in the drawings opens the cut list with it selected: …/cut-list?part=leg-front. The cut list's part name links back to …/diagrams?part=leg-front. The drawing and its row are no longer on screen together, which is the main cost of this direction (see question 3).
  • The summary's flags link to …/structural-review and focus the expert checks heading.
  • A build step's piece opens …/diagrams?part=… at that part's cut detail; Back returns to the step.
Edit and "Saved."Edit sits in the header on every section. The edit link carries the section you came from (…/edit?from=cut-list), and Save returns there with "Saved." under the header. Cancel returns there too. The preview itself is unchanged.
PrintEvery section stays in the page; on screen all but the current one are hidden, and the print stylesheet shows them all, each starting a page with its title block as today. The browser's own Print menu gives the same result, since nothing is loaded on demand.
Accessibility
  • After moving, focus goes to the section's heading, which is already focusable (#119). The page title names the section and project: "Structural review · Backyard swing set · Kerf".
  • The contents entry for the current section has aria-current="page". Previous and Next sit in a nav labeled "Plan sections", stacked on the phone as GOV.UK does for screen magnifier users.
  • The phone's bar is a button with aria-expanded that says "Structural review, 3 of 8, contents".
  • Loss: browser find (Cmd+F) only searches the section on screen.
At the desk
The contents are always beside you and one click takes you anywhere, so "stay on the part you care about" is the default: the cut list fills the screen with nothing below it pulling you on. Checking a margin against an input means two sections, but the review shows its inputs in "Show the working", so it rarely comes up.
On the couch
Next and Previous suit reading the plan through on the phone, and the bar always says where you are. Jumping costs two taps.
Against the principles
Keeps order A on screen and paper, the Stage 1 rule. NN/g's advice against splitting linear content (Nielsen 2013) is for articles read start to finish; the plan is consulted a part at a time, the case where Schade (2017) says separate pages may serve better than in-page links. Sections stay whole, so nothing a reader compares is split across tabs, except the drawing and its cut list row.
Cost to build
Small. The sections, contents, and focus handling exist; this adds the routes, the Previous and Next block, the phone's bar, and a print rule. No new data.

The closest fit to the complaint with the least new structure. Its weak point is that a part's drawing and its cut list row sit on different screens.

Direction P2

The summary as a hub, with five views named by question

The plan opens on the summary: the key results, the lowest margin, the flags, the Shopping and Building cards, and a card for each view with its one-line digest. Each card opens its view on its own screen, with "‹ Summary" to go back. The eight sections become five views named for what you are asking, so the drawings and the cut list share a screen. On a phone the cards are a list that drills down, as a settings app does.

Getting there
  • Laptop: one click from the summary to a view; two to another view (Summary, then its card), or one with Next and Previous between views.
  • Phone: the same, as a list. Back to the summary is one tap.
AddressThe summary is #/projects/<id>; each view is …/sound, …/cut, …/buy, …/build-steps, …/entered. History as in P1.
Links between sections
  • The drawings and the cut list share a view, so selecting a part works as it does today (#91) and the selection still goes in the address so a reload keeps it.
  • The summary's flags open "Is it sound?" at the expert checks. A build step's piece opens "What do I cut?" with the piece selected.
Edit and "Saved."As P1: Save returns to the view you came from, with "Saved." under its header.
PrintAs P1, but grouping by question moves the diagrams after the structural review, so the printed order becomes summary, structural review, diagrams, cut list, boards, and so on. That changes order A, which Stage 1 chose, unless the screen and paper orders are allowed to differ, which Stage 1 ruled out.
AccessibilityHub cards are links in a list, so a screen reader hears "list, 5 items". Focus and page titles as P1. The "‹ Summary" link is first in each view, so it is also the quickest way out by keyboard.
At the desk
The hub uses the laptop's width for navigation and gives no contents beside a view, so going from the review to the cut list is a trip through the summary. NN/g's hub pattern fits sessions with one task (Budiu 2015); planning at the desk moves between several parts.
On the couch
The strongest of the three on the phone: the summary answers most questions, and the digest under each view says whether it is worth opening.
Against the principles
Grouping keeps the drawings beside the cut list, which NN/g's tabs guidance argues for: do not split content people compare (Sunwall 2024). The question names have strong scent, but they are a second set of names for the same sections, and they change order A.
Cost to build
Medium. New views that combine sections, digests from core, and a reordered print.

Best on the phone and for a first look. Slower on the laptop, and it reopens Stage 1's section order.

Direction P3

One page, with the sections closed

The page stays as it is, but only the summary starts open. Each other section is a closed header with its digest; opening one leaves the rest as they are. The contents link opens and moves to a section. Open all and Close all sit above the first section. The smallest change, but you still scroll once two or three are open.

Considered

Directions from the issue not drawn on their own

Section tabs

Tabs for the plan's sections on the laptop and a menu on the phone. With eight sections the tabs either wrap or scroll sideways, and NN/g advises as few tabs as possible with one- or two-word labels (Sunwall 2024); "Parameters and inputs" and "Structural review" are not. On the phone the menu is P1's bar. On the laptop a row of tabs under the project name looks like IA-1's Plan, Shop, and Build tabs, which the owner rejected. So tabs are P1 drawn sideways, with more to fit and a look the owner has turned down.

Sections grouped by question

Drawn as part of P2, since grouping and a hub answer different things: grouping is how many screens there are, a hub is how you move between them. Grouping could also go with P1's contents (five entries instead of eight). The one grouping that clearly helps is the drawings with the cut list; question 3 asks about that on its own.

Smaller questions

Two choices inside the direction

Asked for P1, the suggested direction. Question 3 applies to P3 as well.

2. The contents on a phone

a. A bar that opens the listSuggested

"3 of 8 · Structural review" at the top of every section; tapping it opens the list in place. Always says where you are and costs one line. NN/g found many people missed a collapsed contents that floats over the page (Wang and Brown 2023); this one stays in the flow at the top, under the project name, where people look first.

b. The list at the top of every section

GOV.UK's guides do this: the numbered parts list above each part's content. Nothing to open, but eight lines push the section down a screen on every move.

3. Diagrams and the cut list

a. Two sections, part in the addressSuggested

A part in a drawing opens the cut list with that row selected, and the row's name opens the drawing with the part selected. One click each way, Back returns, and order A is unchanged. The drawings get a whole screen, which the isometric and site views need.

b. One "Drawings and cuts" section

Diagrams, cut list, and boards on one screen, as in P2, so a selection shows in both at once. Order A becomes seven sections, and this one is the longest in the plan, which brings back the scrolling for the part of the plan used most.

Recommendation

P1, with the digests in the summary

P1 answers the complaint directly: each section gets the screen to itself, the contents say where you are, and one click or two taps reach any section. It keeps order A on screen and on paper, keeps IA-2's modes as they are, and reuses what #119 built. Its cost, the drawing and its cut list row on separate screens, is met by carrying the part in the address (question 3, a).

From P2, take the one thing it does best on the phone: the summary lists the sections with their digests ("Structural review: lowest margin 1.1x, 6 expert checks"), each linking to its section. On the couch the summary then works as a hub, while the contents and Next keep the laptop quick. The digests come from core, like the key results.

P2 is the better choice if the phone is where the plan is mostly read. P3 is the fallback if P1 turns out to feel like too many screens; it can be built later on top of the same sections.

Not checked: how long each section is on a phone today, and whether Safari opens closed sections for find in P3. Neither changes the comparison.

Precedents and guidance

Sources behind these directions

Read in October 2026. Where a page could not be loaded, the note says so.

NN/g: Scrolling and Attention

In 2018, 57% of viewing time was above the fold and 74% in the first two screens. On today's plan, the build steps and parameters sit many screens down. Basis for giving each section its own top of screen.

Fessenden, 2018

NN/g: Pagination and View All

Articles read start to finish should almost never be split across pages; pagination suits listings. The plan is consulted a part at a time rather than read through, which is why P1 splits it by section and not into arbitrary pages.

Nielsen, 2013

NN/g: In-page links

In-page links help long pages, more so on small screens, but are less useful when people do not read in order; then splitting into separate pages may be better. Back after a contents jump should return to where you were. Basis for P1 and for the history rules.

Schade, 2017

NN/g: Table of contents

A sticky contents in a side rail, with the current section highlighted; on mobile, move it into the body. A floating, collapsible contents on mobile was often missed. Basis for P1's sidebar and question 2.

Wang and Brown, 2023

NN/g: Accordions on desktop

Accordions suit content scanned selectively and hurt content read in full; collapsed panels often do not print, so open them for print. Basis for P3 and its print rule.

Wang, 2023

NN/g: Tabs, used right

As few tabs as possible, short labels, and no tabs for content people compare. Why section tabs are not drawn, and why P2 groups the drawings with the cut list.

Sunwall, 2024

NN/g: Mobile navigation patterns

A hub suits apps used for one task per session, and costs a trip back to the hub for each move. Basis for P2 and its weakness at the desk. NN/g's separate hub-and-spoke article returned "not found".

Budiu, 2015

GOV.UK multi-part guides

A numbered contents list, one part per page with its own address, Next naming the next part, and a printable version of the whole guide at its own address. The model for P1. Read on a live guide; GOV.UK's content-type page did not describe guides.

Vehicle tax Direct Debit guide

GOV.UK Design System: pagination

The block style for content split across pages: Previous and Next labeled with the neighbouring pages' titles, stacked for screen magnifier users, after the content. P1's Previous and Next.

Pagination

GOV.UK step by step navigation

For journeys done in order with a clear start and end, not for reading. Its research found people frustrated moving back and forth between step by steps. Why the plan's sections are not presented as numbered steps; that job is Building mode's.

Step by step navigation

Accessible client-side routing

User testing with screen reader, voice, and magnifier users: after a route change, focus on the new heading worked best of the options tried for screen reader users; resetting to the top of the app was overwhelming; a live region naming the page helped. Basis for moving focus to the section heading and naming it in the title.

Sutton, 2019

WAI-ARIA tabs pattern

Tab list, tabs, and panels, with arrow keys between tabs. Read for the tabs direction; not needed by the suggested one.

APG: Tabs

Paprika recipe manager

One recipe on one screen: ingredients on the left, directions below or beside, with your place in the directions highlighted. A recipe is short enough for one screen; a plan of eight sections is not, which is the difference this issue is about. Paprika's iOS help does not say whether the phone layout changes.

Paprika iOS help · Podfeet review

Woodsmith plans

A published plan lists exploded views, a cutting diagram, and a materials list among its contents, as one printed document. Kerf's printed plan stays one document in order A whichever direction is chosen. Ana White's plan pages could not be loaded (402).
