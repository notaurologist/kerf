The cut list, quick to read at the saw

Each row of the cut list holds a part's name, stock (nominal and actual), length, both end cuts with the saw setting and the angle to the edge, holes, quantity, and long notes, and the text runs together. Two directions follow, drawn with the swing set's worked example and checked against the cabinet's, on the phone, the laptop, and paper. Every length and angle stays core's text (hard rule 1).

What to decide

  1. Which direction: C1 (suggested) a narrow table with each part's details one tap away, or C2 a card per part.
  2. Whether the end cuts show as two small boxes ("20.0° · 70.0°") or as words ("Saw at 20.0°, 70.0° to the edge").

What a woodworker reads at the saw

A printed cut list in a magazine plan is a narrow table: part, quantity, and the three sizes (thickness, width, length), with notes keyed by letter below it. At the saw the questions are which part, how many, how long, and how each end is cut. Reference face, edge angle, holes, and marking instructions are read once, at the bench, before cutting. So the row carries the four answers, and the rest moves one step away.

C1 · A narrow table; details open under the row

Suggested
  • Columns: letter and part, quantity, stock (nominal; actual under it, smaller), length (large, tabular figures), end cuts as two boxed saw settings.
  • Tapping a row opens its details beneath: the cut detail drawing, the angle to the edge, reference face, holes, marks and notes. One row open at a time; the part stays selected in Diagrams.
  • On paper every row prints open.

C2 · A card per part

  • Each part is a card: letter and name, quantity, a large length, the two end cuts drawn as small saw-setting boxes, then the notes in a quiet line, with the cut detail beside it on the laptop.
  • Reads like Building mode's cut card, so the plan and the shop share one look.
