Kerf corners

Issue #148. The owner finds the app's rounded corners too soft for a woodworking tool. This page shows three sharper directions next to today's corners, on the app's real components: each sample uses the app's own styles, and only the corners and line ends change. Each is shown light and dark; narrow the window to see the phone layout.

Numbers are from the swing set's worked example. The drawing is the foot of a back leg (part B), not to scale, with its end cut's 70.0° to the edge.

Decided, October 2026: eased. The app, the drawings, and every icon now use eased corners; this page keeps the comparison as it was drawn. The reasons and rejected directions are in docs/design/README.md, Stage 6.

The two closest directions side by side, each with the tool icons drawn to match: square corners with #86's chosen square icons, and eased corners with the same drawings eased as round 3 did (rounded line ends and joins, a 0.6-unit radius on each box). Critique

DirectionForAgainst
Square Matches the tool icons the owner chose (#86): square ends, sharp joins, a 1-unit line. Reads as a shop drawing: drafting standards (ASME Y14.2) draw boxes, title blocks, and callouts with square corners, and published woodworking plans do the same. GOV.UK and IBM Carbon, both built for plain, task-first use, set their buttons' radius to 0. One value (--radius: 0) and four literal radii change; screens change nothing. Prints and renders the same in every browser. Next to the 3 px focus ring, a square button reads heavier than a rounded one at the same size. Cards in a grid with square corners rely more on spacing to separate them, which the layout already has (Stage 2).
Eased Sharp at a glance, with the corner just softened, as a sanded edge is. At 2 px the difference from square is hard to see on a phone at arm's length; it shows mostly on large boxes (the dialog, cards) on the laptop. Barely different from square on a phone at arm's length. Agrees with the icons only if the tool icons move to their eased drawings (compared above), which round the ends of every short line, such as a tape's tick marks.
Chamfered The most distinctively "woodworking" of the three: a chamfered edge is a real shop detail. With CSS corner-shape: bevel, the border, background, and focus ring are all cut at 45°. corner-shape is in Chrome 139 and later but only in Safari's preview (MDN browser data, October 2026), so on the owner's iPhone it needs the clip-path fallback. That fallback clips everything outside the box, so the 3 px focus ring, drawn 2 px outside, disappears completely, and each corner leaves a gap in the border (checked in Chrome by forcing the fallback; see "Focused" in Safari). Losing the focus ring breaks the focus rule (#43). Holding the shape takes extra rules on every bordered component, against "one place" in the issue. At 3 to 4 px the chamfer is close to invisible on the small tags.

Line ends and joins in drawings and icons

Whatever the corners, the drawings and Kerf's icons should agree with them and with the tool icons. Square ends (butt) stop each line exactly where it is drawn, as on a drafting sheet; round ends add half the line width past each end and soften every corner of a part, such as the leg's foot. Mitered joins keep the corner sharp; on angles sharper than about 29° they fall back to beveled (a miter limit of 4), so a thin sliver never draws a long spike. The text halo in drawings keeps its round join under any direction, since a mitered halo spikes around the letters.

The redrawn icons (flag, info, error, done, pending, chevron, connection) use a 1-unit line on their 16-unit grid: 1.06 px at 17 px text, between the tool icons (0.83 px at 20 px) and the drawings' visible line (1.3 px). The circles stay: error and info are conventional round signs, and a square frame reads as a checkbox.

The part letter

The part letter stays a filled box in the text color, and its corners follow the direction in both places it appears: beside the part's name and in the drawing (Diagram.tsx draws it with rx = s / 5 today).

Recommendation

Square or eased; choose by look. Both work the same on the iPhone, the laptop, and paper, and both change in one place. Square matches the tool icons as chosen in #86. Eased softens the large boxes a little and takes the tool icons to their eased drawings, so icons and boxes still agree. The owner leans eased (October 2026). Chamfered is not ready: on the iPhone it loses the focus ring.

If square is chosen, the change is: --radius: 0, the four literal radii (tags, part letter, dialog, catalog panel) removed, square ends and mitered joins on .k-shape, the drawing's letter box with no rx, and Kerf's icons redrawn as above. If eased is chosen: --radius: 2px, 1 px on tags, 2 px on the part letter and dialog, round joins on .k-shape, the letter box with a small rx, and the tool icons and Kerf's icons drawn with round ends and joins. Either way, screens need no changes, since they write no styles.
