Tool icons

A small icon for each tool type in the library (#85), shown beside the tool's name in the tool list, in each method's list of tools, and in Building mode. No open-license set covers woodworking tools: Lucide, for example, has a drill, a hammer, and a shovel, but no saw, clamp, or level. So Kerf draws its own, in the repo, as it does its other icons (#79).

Round 1 drew three directions on 10 tools; the owner leaned toward A, outlines, and found all three much too chunky. Round 2 drew lighter outlines; still too chunky, and too rounded. Round 3 draws thinner lines again, and sharp corners: square line ends and joins, and no rounding on the tools' bodies, with a slightly eased version beside it. Circles stay round where the tool is round: blades, hubs, and nuts. The panels use Kerf's C1 colors (#43) and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (#42).

Decided, October 2026

The owner chose square corners, a 1-unit line, after three rounds: round 1's three directions were too chunky (the owner leaned toward A, outlines), round 2's lighter outlines were still too chunky and too rounded, and round 3 narrowed to a 1-unit line, square or eased. Shared edges are drawn once. The full set is below; the icons appear beside each tool's name in the tool list, each method's Tools line, and Building mode. Whether Kerf's other icons and the rest of the app follow is #148. Reasons and rejected rounds are in docs/design/README.md.

What changed from round 2

Thinner, and sharp

  • Thinner lines. Round 2 compared 1.25 to 1.75 units. Round 3 compares 1 and 1.25, lighter than the text beside them (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next's regular stem is 0.084 of the type size, measured from Kerf's font file: 1.4 px in 17 px text).
  • Sharp corners. Round 2's round line ends, round joins, rounded boxes, and pill-shaped handles read as soft and app-like. Square line ends and sharp joins read as drafted, like a shop drawing. The drill's body is a box now, and the chisel's handle is a square-ended handle with chamfered corners.
  • Sizes. At these weights a line at 16 px is under a pixel and goes gray, so the icons are for 20 px (the tool list) and 24 px (Building mode) on screen. In print they are vector and stay sharp at any size.

A 1-unit line is 0.83 px at 20 px and 1 px at 24 px: under two device pixels on a 2x (Retina) screen, where it stays crisp; on a 1x monitor it goes slightly gray. Round 3 also tried 1.25 units; the owner preferred 1.

For the chosen weight

Construction guide

It goes into docs/design/system.md with the set, so tools added later match.

  • Grid: 24 × 24 units, artwork inside a one-unit margin.
  • Line: one weight for every line, 1 unit, centered. Square: butt line ends and mitered joins; eased: round ends and joins. Outlines only; nothing filled.
  • Corners: square boxes and handles; a 45° chamfer where a real tool has one (the chisel's handle, the combination square's head). Circles only for round parts.
  • View: side-on and flat, no perspective; long tools (chisel) at 45°.
  • Shared edges: where two parts touch, draw the edge once. A part that butts against another is an open path ending on the other's outline, never a second outline along it.
  • Detail: only what tells the tool from its neighbors: the blade and what it sits on, the 45° face, the fence, the nose of a driver. At least 1.5 units between parallel lines so they stay apart at 20 px.
  • Size in use: 20 px beside desk text, 24 px in shop density, about 14 px in print; never under 20 px on screen. Always beside the tool's name, hidden from screen readers.
  • Code: one component in apps/web/src/ui keyed by tool-type ID, shown on the catalog page.

Precedents and guidance

Sources

Checked in October 2026.

Labels

NN/g: few icons are recognized universally (home, print, search), so a text label should sit beside an icon, visible without hovering. Test icons by asking people what they expect each to mean.

Icon Usability

Weight and consistency

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: highly simplified designs; one size, level of detail, stroke weight, and perspective across an app's icons; icon weight matched to the text beside it. SF Symbols comes in nine weights, one for each weight of the system font.

Icons · SF Symbols

Grids and strokes

Material Design's system icons: a 24 dp grid and one 2 dp stroke throughout. Lucide: a 24 × 24 canvas, at least 1 px of padding, 2 px round-capped strokes. Both are heavier than round 2; Lucide lets its users set a lighter stroke.

Material: system icons · Lucide: design principles

Open sets

Lucide has drill, hammer, shovel, ruler, and wrench, but no saw, clamp, or level (checked in its repository, October 2026).

lucide-icons/lucide

Testing symbols

ANSI Z535.3 sets 85% correct understanding, with under 5% critical confusions, as the bar for a safety symbol used without words. Tool icons here always have their name, so they need not meet it; it shows how far an unlabeled pictogram has to go.
