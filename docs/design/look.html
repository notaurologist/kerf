Design milestone · #226 · Stage 10

Another look: color, type, and the key screens

Every screen now exists, and the frame from Stage 9 is built. This round asks whether the key screens look like one finished product, and proposes three directions that go progressively further from what is built today. Each is drawn on the same four screens, in light and dark, and measured.

What I'd like you to decide

  1. How far to go. D1 changes surfaces and hierarchy and keeps the Stage 4 colors. D2 does all of D1 and takes the blue off actions. D3 does all of D1 with a new palette and a graphite band. Suggested: D2.
  2. The fixes that apply whichever you pick (below the directions): the mode bar in dark, the mode bar on a phone, and the repeated shopping guide. Suggested: take all three.
  3. Anything to take from a direction you don't pick. For example D3's larger figures, or its end-cut corner on the primary button.

What is weak today

I went through the key screens in the running app at 1280 and 390 px, in light and dark, with two projects made from the catalog's sample values. Ordered from most to least visible:

  1. Everything is a box. The summary's five figures, the two mode cards, each structural check, each project, and the flags all sit in bordered boxes of the same weight, so nothing leads. Boxes are for collections of unlike things you can act on (NN/g, "Cards: UI-Component Definition", 2016); here most are lists of like things, which rules and space separate with less ink (Tufte's "smallest effective difference", Visual Explanations, 1997; Refactoring UI, "Use fewer borders", 2018).
  2. The plan's headline numbers are body size. On the summary, "120"", "109-1/4"" and "margin 0.8x" are 17 px bold, the same size as their labels' neighbours, so the summary reads as a form rather than the answer.
  3. Blue is on almost everything. Primary and secondary buttons, every link in a list, part names in the cut list, check names, every disclosure, input needed, the changed bar and the ticks. In dark, the light-blue primary button is the brightest thing on a phone screen. Stage 4 chose "one blue" so that the few things that need attention stand out; it now marks too much to do that.
  4. In dark, Shopping and Building open with a chalk-white band. The mode bar is drawn in the text color, so in dark it turns light: the brightest thing on the screen in a dim garage, at the top, where the eye starts. The theme menu inside it does not invert, so it sits as a dark box on the light band.
  5. The mode bar takes two rows on a phone. At 390 px the theme menu and Exit wrap under the mode's name, which costs about 60 px of a Building step above the first length.
  6. Shopping repeats its guide on every line. "The boards, and what to look for" is a link under each lumber line, so the list alternates item, link, item, link.
  7. The brand is only in the corner. The wordmark is graphite and the brand rules keep it out of blue (brand.md), while the screens are mostly blue. The end cut that defines the mark appears nowhere else.

Type and density held up: the Stage 3 scale and Atkinson Hyperlegible read well on the newer screens (glossary, tool pages, the new-project questions), and shop density now applies its space and 20 px text (#202). The proposals below change sizes only for the plan's figures and, in D3, the headings.

Glare lays a veil of white over the mock-ups, as light reflected off a screen does.

The directions, critiqued

D1 · Drawing sheet: rules instead of boxes, larger figures, Stage 4's colors

What changes

  • Figures, modes, checks and projects become ruled rows: a 2 px graphite rule above a group, hairlines between its rows, no boxes. Boxes stay for the things that are different in kind: flags, the Resume card, fields.
  • The summary's figures go from 17 to 24 px bold (the scale's step 2), labels above them in 14 px.
  • One primary button per screen: on the summary, Start shopping stays primary and Start building becomes secondary until shopping has begun. Today both are primary, side by side.
  • Rows that are links (a project, a mode) are named in the text color with a chevron, not underlined. Links inside sentences stay blue and underlined.
  • Colors are unchanged, so tokens.test.ts keeps every ratio.

For

  • The headline numbers lead. It is how a drawing's title block and a printed cut list separate things: rules and alignment.
  • The smallest change. Mostly components.css; no token values move.
  • Underlines stay where GOV.UK and WCAG 1.4.1 want them: on links in running text.

Against

  • Blue is still on every action, so the "too much blue" problem only shrinks by the links it removes.
  • In dark, the light-blue primary button stays the brightest thing on a phone.
  • Without boxes, rows need the hairline to stay distinct in glare: hairlines are 1.9 to 2.8:1 under 20% glare, so the 2 px group rule and spacing carry the structure.

D2 · Pencil and chalk line: D1, with actions in graphite and blue kept for attention

What changes

  • All of D1.
  • Primary buttons are graphite with paper text (chalk with graphite text in dark). Secondary buttons are outlined in graphite. Links are the text color, underlined.
  • Blue is kept for exactly four jobs: input needed, focus, where you are (the current place and section), and dimensions in drawings. That is Stage 4's "one blue" applied to attention, not to action.
  • Ticks are graphite. The changed bar on state tags moves to the strong border gray.

For

  • The screens and the brand become one product: the wordmark is graphite (brand.md), and so are the actions.
  • The most legible under glare: the action color is the text color, 4.6:1 at 20% glare in light against 3.3:1 for blue.
  • Input needed is now the only blue in a form, so it stands out as Stage 5a meant it to.
  • Only the role tokens #219 split out change, which is what they were split for.

Against

  • A link in running text is the text color with an underline; it relies on the underline alone. WCAG 1.4.1 accepts this, but it is less obvious than blue (GOV.UK keeps blue).
  • A graphite primary button is the heaviest thing on a light screen; one per screen, as today, keeps that in check.
  • The changed bar in gray is quieter than in blue (4.4:1 light, 5.1:1 dark), and it also has its label.

D3 · Workshop: D1, with a warmer paper, an indigo chalk line, a graphite band, and the end cut

What changes

  • All of D1.
  • A new palette: kraft paper (warmer and a little darker), ink text with a brown cast, indigo in place of blue. Flags get a stronger amber fill so they still stand off the warmer paper.
  • The title bar and the laptop sidebar are a graphite band in both themes, as the mode bar is.
  • Headings move to a 1.25 ratio (17, 21, 26, 33 px) and figures to 26 px.
  • The primary button's top-right corner is cut at 45°, the k's end cut.

For

  • The most character: it reads as a workshop tool, not a web page.
  • The band ties the frame to the mode bar, so planning and shop screens share one shape.
  • The end cut carries the brand into the screens.

Against

  • The darker paper costs contrast: text on page falls from 16.1 to 15.1:1 in light, and to 4.3:1 under 20% glare, below the 4.5:1 that C1 kept.
  • Brown-cast text and kraft paper are close to the amber flag; the fill had to get stronger to stay distinct, which makes flags louder.
  • The cut corner must be drawn behind the label, or it clips the focus ring; and a motif on every button risks reading as decoration. On the wordmark it is one detail; repeated, it is a style.
  • A whole new palette re-opens Stage 4 and every diagram tone, which were measured together. The largest build of the three.

Suggested: D2

It fixes the two biggest problems, nothing leads and blue marks everything, with the least new risk: D1's surfaces plus a change to role tokens that #219 made for this purpose. It brings the screens in line with the brand and is the strongest under glare. D3's character is real, but most of it comes from the band and the cut corner, which can be tried on top of D2 later; its palette costs glare contrast for warmth.

Fixes that apply whichever direction you pick

  1. The mode bar in dark. Graphite in light, as now; in dark, the card color with a hairline under it, instead of chalk. The mock-ups for D1 to D3 show it. The "Today" mock-up shows the current band.
  2. The mode bar on a phone. One row: the mode's name (cut short with an ellipsis if needed), a Menu button holding the theme, and Exit. This matches the planning frame's Menu, and the mode is still named twice (the bar and the step's own heading), as the mode rules require (NN/g on modes).
  3. Shopping's guide once per group. One "What to look for in lumber" link under the Lumber heading instead of one under each line.

None changes a number or the order of anything. Each one changes how the shop modes read, so the next garage check (#69, #70) should look at the mode bar in dark and the one-row bar.

Measured contrast

WCAG 2.2 contrast ratios for each direction's pairs, light and dark, and with 20% glare, using the same method as color.html: the glare veil adds 20% of white's luminance to both colors. Every pair meets its minimum without glare. Under glare, no direction holds secondary text or blue at 4.5:1; Stage 4's rule 5 already keeps secondary text out of the shop modes.
