Usability pass · #362 · for the owner's pick

Build steps, not a wall of text

Each step lists its operations, and each operation its method, skills, flags, and tool suggestions, all as paragraphs. When no method fits the tools, the same long "No method fits your tools…" sentence repeats for every cut of a board. Two directions make a step quick to take in: what to do first, the parts and measurements, then the method detail. Expert checks sit on the step they are about (#356). Every flag still shows on its step.

What to decide

  1. Which direction: B1 (suggested) a step as a short sequence with the method folded, or B2 a step as a drawing and a parts table.
  2. Whether a missing tool is said once per step ("Your tools have no saw for these cuts: add one in My toolbox") instead of once per operation.

B1 · Do this, with these, then how

Suggested
  • The step opens with one line of what to do ("Cut the beam from board 1"), then the pieces with their lengths and saw settings as a short list.
  • Operations of one kind on one board fold into one line: "2 square crosscuts · miter saw". The method's text opens under it.
  • Expert checks follow the parts, in their flag style, each with what to ask and why.
  • Same in the plan and in Building mode; Building mode adds the ticks and the drawing beside it from 600 px.

B2 · A drawing and a parts table first

  • The step's drawing leads (the board for a cutting step, the action drawing for assembly), then a small table of parts, then the operations as in today's list.
  • Closest to printed plans, which lead each step with a drawing (Agrawala et al. 2003, already the basis of Building mode's drawings).
