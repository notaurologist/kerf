Kerf · Design milestone · Issue #39

Wireframes for planning, shopping, and building

Three places Kerf gets used, with three directions for each, drawn low-fidelity. Every number in them is real: it comes from the worked example in snapshots/v2.json (beam 120", height 96", legs splayed 20°, 4x6 beam, 4x4 legs, 1" of ground slope, legs buried 24"), printed through core's own formatters. Text written [like this] is a placeholder for content that does not exist yet, such as build-step method text from M6.

The wireframes decide structure and behavior only. Grey boxes, the system font, and the blue notes are not visual-design choices; type and color come in #42 and #43.

Laptop and tablet frames are wider than a phone screen. On a phone, scroll them sideways inside their box.

What I need from you

Eight choices. My suggestion is listed first in each, but they are yours. Reply in the chat or on the pull request with something like L: B+A, S: A+B, G: A, Q1 a, Q2 a, Q3 a, Q4 a, Q5 a, or tell me what to change.

  1. Laptop, planning: B for the project page and A for the edit screen, or one of A, B, C alone.
  2. Phone at the store: A with each lumber row opening into B's board card, or A, B, C alone.
  3. Phone or tablet in the garage: A, with cutting steps ordered board by board (C) and each piece opening a cut card (B).
  4. Live preview while editing: dry-run route, core in the browser, or no preview.
  5. Saved progress in the shop: on the server, on the device, or none.
  6. Linking parts across views: ids on every diagram line, per-part details only, or printed callouts only.
  7. Grouping cuts: by board in the shop, by part, or by saw setting.
  8. When the connection drops: queue changes in memory, keep a copy on the device, or nothing beyond today.

Below the wireframes: the precedents each direction follows, and the 21 requirements they imply, each marked met or not met by today's core, snapshot, and API.

Feedback and visibility of system status (Norman; Nielsen's first heuristic)
The effect of every change appears next to its cause. "What does 2° more splay do to the leg lengths and the bending margin?" is answered without leaving the field.
Two states of the same numbers
A preview and a saved plan on one screen invite confusing them. The preview label, the change marks, and Save/Discard have to make the state obvious. Hard rule 6 decides where preview numbers come from (Q1).
Room for long content
The output pane is about 600 px wide. The cut list fits; the structural working (formulas, factors, sources) is cramped and reads better full width.
Print
A split screen does not print. It still needs a document view for paper.

Open questions

Five choices the wireframes depend on

Each has a suggestion, outlined in blue, and alternatives. Options that would change a recorded decision say which one.

Q1 · Live preview while editing

L-A shows output updating before saving. The API has no route that generates without saving.

a · Dry-run routeSuggested

A route such as POST /api/projects/:id/preview runs core on the server and returns the snapshot and its differences from the saved one, without saving. Called a moment after typing stops.

Keeps hard rule 6 and ARCHITECTURE principle 2 as they are. Costs one round trip per change, and a cold start on Vercel can make the first one slow.

b · Core in the browser

The web app calls core's generate for the preview only and labels it; saving still goes through the API.

Instant. Changes hard rule 6 and ARCHITECTURE principle 2, so it needs a decision record. A tab left open across a deploy would preview with the older template while the server saves with the newer one.

c · No preview

Save, then see L-B's "Changed by this edit" summary.

No new API. Each trial of a value takes a save and a look; a bad trial must be edited back.

Q2 · Saved progress in the shop

S-A ticks shopping lines; G-A, G-B, and G-C track steps, cuts, and boards. Nothing stores progress today.

a · On the serverSuggested

A project_progress table: project, kind (shopping line, board, part instance, step), key, time done. Seen on every device. When a project regenerates, entries whose key still exists with the same numbers stay; the rest clear, and the page says how many reset and why.

New table, routes, and tests. Needs stable keys for boards, part instances, and steps (R4, R5, R14).

b · On the device

Ticks kept in the browser's storage.

Conflicts with decision 0005 (no browser-only storage of user data), and Safari can clear it after 7 days. The laptop and phone would disagree.

c · None

Ticks last while the page is open.

No new state. A reload or a sleeping tab loses the place; S-C and G-B fit this best.

Q3 · Linking parts across views

Tap a cut list row and the part lights up in a diagram (L-C), or a step names the parts it uses (G-A). The cut list and board plan already share partId.

a · Ids everywhereSuggested

Every diagram line, hardware location, flag, and step carries the part id and instance it concerns. Views can link any way later without changing the snapshot again.

Adding ids now costs little. Adding them after M5 costs a new snapshot shape, an upgrade, and a fixture (0014).

b · Per-part details only

Cut details are drawn per part and linked from the cut list. The isometric and exploded views carry no ids.

Less data. Rules out highlighting a part in the assembled views.

c · Printed callouts

Each part gets a letter (A for the beam, B for the back legs…) shown in diagrams and the cut list. No interaction.

Works on paper, which a and b also need. Can sit on top of a.

Q4 · Grouping cuts

The cut list is grouped by part; the board plan already says which pieces come from which board. In this design every angled cut is a 20.0° saw setting.

a · By board in the shop, by part in the planSuggested

Planning keeps today's cut list by part. Cutting steps go board by board (G-C).

Boards need ids (R4). The board data itself is in the snapshot.

b · By part everywhere

One grouping in every view.

Simplest. In the shop you look up the board for each part yourself.

c · By saw setting

All square cuts, then all 20.0° cuts, so the saw is set once per angle.

Fewest setups. Needs operations per part (M6), and mixes boards, so you handle each board twice.

Q5 · When the connection drops

Today an open project stays readable: the project page loads one response and fetches nothing else. A reload with no connection fails (0005).

a · Queue changes in memorySuggested

Keep today's model. Steps and diagrams arrive in the same project response. Ticks made offline wait in memory and retry, with "2 changes not saved yet" in view until they go through.

No decision changes. A reload while offline still loses the queued ticks, and the page says so.

b · Keep a copy on the device

Store the open project and queued ticks in the browser so a reload offline still works.

This is the read-only offline mode PRODUCT.md lists after the MVP. Changes 0005.

c · Nothing beyond today

Reading keeps working; ticks fail with an error while offline.

Only fits if Q2 is c.

Precedents and guidance

Where these directions come from

Each direction follows a pattern that already works elsewhere. The sources were checked in October 2026; the notes say what each one supports and where it does not apply.

Woodsmith plans: materials list and cutting diagram

Woodsmith's published plans ship a materials list with a cutting diagram that draws each part on the board it comes from. That is S-B and G-C. Readers note the diagrams work as a guide, since real boards vary, so the by-part cut card (G-B) stays available.

Creating cutting diagrams · LumberJocks discussion

Recipe sites' cook mode

Recipe pages offer a "Cook Mode" toggle that keeps the screen awake with the Screen Wake Lock API while you follow the steps. That is G-A's situation: hands busy, phone propped up. Safari on iOS supports the API from 16.4. Some browsers grant it only after a tap, so the shop view should turn it on from a control, not on page load.

WP Recipe Maker cook mode · Chrome: Screen Wake Lock · caniuse

Assembly instructions

Flat-pack instructions break assembly into small numbered steps, each one action, with exploded views and letters that tie parts on the page to parts in the box. That is G-A and Q3 c. They are also known to confuse people when a picture carries everything, which is why G-A keeps words and numbers beside the sketch.

Wordless instructions · Designing assembly instructions without words

Shopping lists grouped by store layout

AnyList groups a list into categories ordered to match a store's aisles, with a separate order per store. That is S-A's grouping. Kerf's two places (lumber yard, hardware aisle) are simpler, so fixed groups are enough.

AnyList: category sets

Offline and pending changes

Google's offline and sync guidelines show sync state only near what will change, and say not to style offline as an error. Expensify's offline guide queues actions and tells the user they have not been sent yet, and never shows unsent changes as saved. That is Q5 a.

Open Health Stack: offline and sync · Expensify: offline UX

Usability principles named in the critiques

Nielsen's ten heuristics (1 visibility of system status, 3 user control, 6 recognition over recall, 8 minimalist design). WCAG 2.2 target sizes: 24 by 24 CSS pixels at AA (2.5.8), 44 by 44 at AAA (2.5.5). The shop views aim above AAA because of gloves.

NN/g: 10 usability heuristics · WCAG 2.5.5 · WCAG 2.5.8
