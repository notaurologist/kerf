Design · Stage 9 · #254

An app frame, motion, loading, and the keyboard

Kerf works, but it feels like a website. You named two causes: navigation is page-style (a row of links in a header, whole pages that scroll) and nothing moves, so changing screens has no direction. This stage draws three frames to hold the screens, a way for screens to move, a gentler way to show loading, and keyboard use on the laptop.

The structure from Stage 1 stays: the project is the plan, and Shopping and Building are full-screen modes. What changes is the frame around the screens, which amends Stage 1's navigation-by-device table.

The demos below are drawn in Kerf's colors, light or dark with your device, but in the system font. Tap the buttons beside each phone to try them. Under the "Reduce motion" setting, each demo behaves as Kerf would.

What to choose

  1. The frame. F1 the header kept, held in place; F2 a tab bar on the phone and one sidebar on the laptop and iPad; or F3 F2's phone with three panes on the laptop. Suggested: F2.
  2. How screens move. M1 a full slide, as iOS pushes a screen; M2 a short shift with a fade; or M3 a fade only. Suggested: M2.
  3. How a screen waits. L1 the old screen holds for a moment, then a skeleton; or L2 a skeleton at once. Either way, a long wait adds a pencil line and a note. Suggested: L1.
  4. The keyboard. K1 arrow keys for sections and steps; or K2 K1 plus a jump-to box on Command-K. Suggested: K1 now.

What is there now

Every planning screen sits under one header: the wordmark, then Projects, Tools, Glossary, the theme menu, and Log out. The header scrolls away with the page. Links change the address and the new screen replaces the old at once, scrolled to the top, with focus on its heading (lib/screenChange.ts). A screen that loads data shows nothing for 300 ms, then "Loading…" until the data arrives (Loading in ui/feedback.tsx). The plan has its contents beside it on the laptop and a "3 of 8" bar on the phone (Stage 1b). Shopping and Building replace the header with a dark mode bar and Exit.

The only motion is a disclosure arrow turning (0.15 s) and the parts sliding between the assembled and exploded drawings (0.4 s, #200).

What stays the same in every direction

  • IA-2: the project is the plan; Shopping and Building fill the screen with only their bar and Exit. No frame shows inside a mode.
  • Addresses and the browser's Back and Forward. Each screen keeps its #/ address, and Back plays the move in reverse.
  • Focus goes to the new screen's heading and the page title names it, after the move ends.
  • Reduced motion: screens swap with a quick fade or at once, and no skeleton shimmers. The reset layer's rule still outranks every component (WCAG 2.3.3).
  • Print gives the whole plan in order A, with no frame.
  • No sideways scrolling at 320 px; shop density keeps its 56 px controls.
  • Styles only in ui/; the frame is a component like any other.
  • No new dependency. Screen moves use the browser's View Transitions (Safari 18, Chrome 111, Firefox 144); an older browser changes screens at once.
  • A screen shows data the API has just sent. Nothing shows an old copy while it waits (#253).

F2 A tab bar on the phone, one sidebar on the laptop and iPad

On the phone, a title bar names the screen and has a back link ("‹ Projects"), and a tab bar at the bottom holds Projects, Tools, and Glossary. Shopping and Building are that modal case: they cover the tab bar, as they cover the header now. From 840 px, the same three places are a sidebar. When a project is open, its sections appear inside the sidebar under its name, so the laptop has one column of navigation, not a sidebar beside a contents list. That is two levels, the most Apple suggests for a sidebar.

How screens move

A move should say where you went. Material names the patterns: a shared axis for screens with "a spatial or navigational relationship" (forward and back, or the sections in order), a fade through for places with no strong relationship (switching tabs), and a fade for things that appear within the screen. Apple asks for motion that is purposeful, brief, optional, and never in the way: "don't make people wait for an animation to complete."

M1

A full slide

The new screen slides the full width over the old, as iOS pushes a screen; 0.35 s. The most familiar from native apps.

Longest travel, so the longest time. NN/g puts most animations at 100 to 500 ms and keeps 400 ms for "big movements across large screens". On a laptop, a 900 px slide is a lot of moving for a click.

M2

A short shift with a fade

The old screen fades as it shifts 24 px; the new one fades in from 24 px the other side; 0.25 s in, 0.2 s out. Material's shared axis, on screens of any width.

Inside NN/g's "200–300 ms" for a substantial screen change. Says direction without moving much, so it works the same on a phone and a laptop.

M3

A fade only

The screens cross-fade; 0.15 s. Smooth, but no direction: forward and back look the same.

This is what reduced motion gets in M1 and M2 anyway.

In every move: ease out, never linear (NN/g). Durations as tokens beside --duration-quick and --duration-move. A tap during a move is not held up: the move ends at once and the next screen starts. Under reduced motion every move is a 0.15 s fade, or none, which the reset layer must cover for the view-transition pseudo-elements as well (they are not matched by *).

How a screen waits

How long screens take, from decision 0006 and re-checked October 31, 2026:

  • 0.12 s with the database awake. Most screens.
  • 2.8 s with it asleep. Neon sleeps after 5 idle minutes, so this is the first screen after any break. In the shop that is common.

NN/g: under a second needs no indicator; a full screen loading for up to 10 seconds suits a skeleton that shows the content's shape (not just the frame). The evidence on skeletons is mixed: in Viget's test (136 people), the skeleton was judged the slowest of a skeleton, a spinner, and a blank screen. So the plan below shows a skeleton only when the wait is long enough to need one.

L1

The old screen holds, then a skeleton

The link you pressed dims, and the old screen stays for up to 0.3 s. If the data arrives in that time, which is most of the time, the new screen moves in complete: no blank, no placeholder. If not, it moves in as a skeleton of its own shape, and the content fades into place.

Native apps and many web frameworks keep the old screen until the next is ready. In code, React's transitions can hold the old screen while the new one loads; the screens' loading (useLoad) would change to fit. More work than L2.

L2

A skeleton at once

The new screen moves in at once as a skeleton, and the content replaces it when it arrives. Simple, and the move starts the moment you tap.

On a fast load (0.12 s) the skeleton shows for a blink before the content, which is the flash Viget's result warns about. Try "Awake" with L2.

A long wait, in either: after 1 s, a pencil line starts along the top, as a line is drawn against a straightedge, slowing as it goes; it makes no claim about how much is left. After 2 s a note says what is happening: "Still loading. The first screen after a few idle minutes takes a few seconds." When the data arrives the line finishes and the content fades in. The skeleton shimmers gently; under reduced motion it is still, there is no line, and the note appears alone. Screen readers hear one "Loading" status, as now.

After 10 s the note changes to the connection problem Kerf shows today, with Try again.

The keyboard on the laptop

Tab, Enter, and Escape already work everywhere (Stage 6). The question is shortcuts for moving. Apple asks for custom shortcuts only for the most common actions, without taking over the system's own. WCAG 2.1.4 asks that a shortcut of a single letter, number, or symbol can be turned off or only works with focus on it, which rules out Gmail-style single keys unless Kerf adds a setting.

K1

Arrow keys

In the plan, Left and Right go to the previous and next section. In Building, Left and Right go to the previous and next step (looking, not marking done). Only when focus is not in a field. Each is shown beside Previous and Next as a small hint.

K2

K1 and a jump-to box

Command-K (Control-K elsewhere) opens a box to type a project, a section, a tool, or a glossary term, and Enter goes there.

Useful once there are many projects and templates. With one or two projects it duplicates the sidebar, so it can wait.

Suggested: F2, M2, L1, K1

F2 answers "page-style navigation" directly and keeps one column of navigation on the laptop. M2 gives every move a direction without much travel, on any screen. L1 means the usual 0.12 s load shows no loading state at all, and the 2.8 s wake-up gets a skeleton, the pencil line, and an honest note. K1 covers the moves you make most, with nothing to remember.

The two bars at the bottom of the phone in Safari are the cost of F2. If they bother you in the garage check, the fix is the home-screen install (amending 0005), not a different frame.

Sources behind these directions

  • Apple, Human Interface Guidelines: Tab bars (navigation not actions; visible except under a modal; iPad tab bar that converts to a sidebar), Sidebars (no more than two levels; split view for deeper), Motion (purposeful, optional, brief, cancellable). Read October 31, 2026.
  • Material Design, Motion: container transform, shared axis (x, y, z), fade through for bottom navigation, fade.
  • Page Laubheimer, Executing UX Animations: Duration and Motion Characteristics, NN/g, 2020: 100–500 ms, 200–300 ms for substantial screen changes, ease out, entering a little longer than exiting.
  • Samhita Tankala, Skeleton Screens 101, NN/g, 2023: none under 1 s, skeletons for full-screen loads under 10 s, frame-only skeletons are not progress indicators.
  • Viget, A Bone to Pick with Skeleton Screens: 136 participants; the skeleton was judged slowest.
  • W3C, Understanding SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA), and SC 2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts (A).
  • MDN, View Transition API; Chrome, Same-document view transitions (types for forward and back); Can I use: Safari 18, Chrome 111, Firefox 144.
